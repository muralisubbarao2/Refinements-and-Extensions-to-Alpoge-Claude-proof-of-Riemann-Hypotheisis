We investigate where Ram Calculus can clarify or refine the approach to the Riemann
Hypothesis described in Alpöge’s announcement and two supplied human-readable reconstructions.
At the author’s request, their substantive claims are treated as working inputs: this is not
an audit of the announced proof, and no full Lean build or independent verification of its
arithmetic machinery is attempted. The strongest applications occur in the analytic interface.
We derive pole-compensated divided-difference representations of the centered Mellin transforms,
identify high derivatives with normalized positive-kernel Ram moment rows, and prove explicit
incomplete-gamma and exponential-tail certificates. A logarithmic-mean balancing rule gives a
smaller sufficient derivative-order constant than the deliberately generous choice in the supplied
reconstruction, without changing the critical exponent. We prove a signed-residual certificate
and an exact balanced-shift formula for weighted prime sums, including the correction required
at prime-power thresholds. Further results organize character-weight residuals, integral Hasse
jets, and trace-aware Gram determinants while preserving the arithmetic information that
arbitrary basis normalization can destroy. The additional reading edition identifies concrete
smoothing and theta-test interfaces: we give derivative-sensitive kernel replacement bounds,
commuting-annihilator compression, and a strict nonvanishing error budget. Reproducible
auxiliary calculations illustrate the advantages and failure cases. The report distinguishes
classical ingredients, application-specific deductions proved here, and proposals requiring new
arithmetic input. No new proof of RH, improvement of the critical line, or replacement of
theta-lift, cohomological, or nonvanishing theorems is claimed.
